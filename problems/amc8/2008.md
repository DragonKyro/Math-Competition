## Problem 3
*Tags: algebra*

If February is a month that contains Friday the $13^{\text{th}}$, what day of the week is February 1?

$\textbf{(A)}\ \text{Sunday} \qquad
\textbf{(B)}\ \text{Monday} \qquad
\textbf{(C)}\ \text{Wednesday} \qquad
\textbf{(D)}\ \text{Thursday}\qquad
\textbf{(E)}\ \text{Saturday} $

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution 1.**

We can go backwards by days, but we can also backwards by weeks. If we go backwards by weeks, we see that February 6 is a Friday. If we now go backwards by days, February 1 is a $\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ \text{Sunday}}$

**Solution 2.**

Since the days of the week repeat every 7 days and $1-13\equiv2\pmod7$, the day of the week of the day 2 days after February 13 is the same as the day of the week of February 1. Since we know that February 13 is a Friday, the day of the week of the day 2 days after February 13 is a Sunday so we get that February 1 is a $\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ \text{Sunday}}$.

**Solution 3.**

We can go back 2 weeks and say that Friday is the -1st of February. Going forward 2 days, we get that February 1st is Sunday. So our answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ \text{Sunday}}$.

</details>

Mr. Harman needs to know the combined weight in pounds of three boxes he wants to mail. However, the only available scale is not accurate for weights less than $100$ pounds or more than $150$ pounds. So the boxes are weighed in pairs in every possible way. The results are $122$, $125$ and $127$ pounds. What is the combined weight in pounds of the three boxes?

$\textbf{(A)}\ 160\qquad
\textbf{(B)}\ 170\qquad
\textbf{(C)}\ 187\qquad
\textbf{(D)}\ 195\qquad
\textbf{(E)}\ 354$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution 1.**

Each box is weighed twice during this, so the combined weight of the three boxes is half the weight of these separate measures:

$$\frac{122+125+127}{2} = \frac{374}{2} = \boxed{\textbf{(C)}\ 187}.$$

**Solution 2.**

Using variables $a$, $b$, and $c$ to denote the boxes, with $a \le b \le c$. It is obvious that $a+b=122$ and $a+c=125$, since these are the two smallest pairs. Subtracting the former equation from the latter results in $c-b=3$. Additionally, $c+b=127$. Solving for $c$ and $b$ gives $c=65$ and $b=62$, so we can thus find $a=60$. Solving $60+62+65=\boxed{\textbf{(C) }\ 187}$

**Solution 3.**

We use variables $x$, $y$, and $z$ to denote the three boxes. We know that $x+y=122$, $x+z=125$, and $y+z=127$. Note that the order of the variables or equations doesn't matter as long as they are consistent. By adding all three equations, we obtain $2x+2y+2z=122+125+127=374$. Dividing be 2, we obtain the sum $x+y+z$ is $\boxed{\textbf{(C) }\ 187}$.

## Problem 15
*Tags: algebra*

In Theresa's first $8$ basketball games, she scored $7, 4, 3, 6, 8, 3, 1$ and $5$ points. In her ninth game, she scored fewer than $10$ points and her points-per-game average for the nine games was an integer. Similarly in her tenth game, she scored fewer than $10$ points and her points-per-game average for the $10$ games was also an integer. What is the product of the number of points she scored in the ninth and tenth games?

$\textbf{(A)}\ 35\qquad
\textbf{(B)}\ 40\qquad
\textbf{(C)}\ 48\qquad
\textbf{(D)}\ 56\qquad
\textbf{(E)}\ 72$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution 1.**

The total number of points from the first $8$ games is $7+4+3+6+8+3+1+5=37$. We have to make this a multiple of $9$ by scoring less than $10$ points. The smallest multiple of $9$ that is greater than $37$ is $45$. $45-37=8$. Now we have to add a number to get a multiple of 10. The next multiple of $10$ is $50$, which means that we add $5$. Multiplying these together, you get $8\cdot5 = $ $\boxed{\textbf{(B)}\ 40}$.

</details>

\textbf{(C)}\ 128\qquad
\textbf{(D)}\ 132\qquad

Instead of finding $n$, we find $x=\frac{n}{3}$. We want $x$ and $9x$ to be three-digit whole numbers. There are $12$ whole numbers in the closed set $\left[100,111\right]$ , so the answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ 12}$.

## Problem 25
*Tags: geometry*

Margie's winning art design is shown. The smallest circle has radius 2 inches, with each successive circle's radius increasing by 2 inches. Which of the following is closest to the percent of the design that is black?

_(Asymptote diagram omitted — see AoPS for image)_

$ \textbf{(A)}\ 42\qquad \textbf{(B)}\ 44\qquad \textbf{(C)}\ 45\qquad \textbf{(D)}\ 46\qquad \textbf{(E)}\ 48\qquad$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution 1.**

Let the smallest circle be 1, the second smallest circle be 2, the third smallest circle be 3, etc. 
$$\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{circle \#} & \text{radius} & \text{area} \\ \hline
1 & 2 & 4\pi \\
2 & 4 & 16\pi \\
3 & 6 & 36\pi \\
4 & 8 & 64\pi \\
5 & 10 & 100\pi \\
6 & 12 & 144\pi
\end{array}$$

The entire circle's area is $144\pi$. The area of the black regions is $(100-64)\pi + (36-16)\pi + 4\pi = 60\pi$. The percentage of the design that is black is $\frac{60\pi}{144\pi} = \frac{5}{12} \approx \boxed{\textbf{(A)}\ 42}$.

</details>
