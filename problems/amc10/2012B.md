## Problem 1
*Tags: straightforward-counting*

Each third-grade classroom at Pearl Creek Elementary has $18$ students and $2$ pet rabbits. How many more students than rabbits are there in all $4$ of the third-grade classrooms?

$ \textbf{(A)}\ 48\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 56\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 64\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 72\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 80 $

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Per classroom.** $18 - 2 = 16$ more students than rabbits.

**Four classrooms.** $4 \cdot 16 = 64$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(C) } 64}$.

</details>

## Problem 6
*Tags: rounding, estimate*

In order to estimate the value of $x-y$ where $x$ and $y$ are real numbers with $x > y > 0$, Xiaoli rounded $x$ up by a small amount, rounded $y$ down by the same amount, and then subtracted her rounded values. Which of the following statements is necessarily correct?


$\textbf{(A) } \text{Her estimate is larger than } x-y \qquad \textbf{(B) } \text{Her estimate is smaller than } x-y \qquad \textbf{(C) } \text{Her estimate equals } x-y \\ \qquad \textbf{(D) } \text{Her estimate equals } y-x \qquad \textbf{(E) } \text{Her estimate is } 0$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Setup.** Let the round-amount be $z > 0$. Xiaoli computed
$$(x + z) - (y - z) = (x - y) + 2z.$$
Since $2z > 0$, this is strictly larger than $x - y$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(A)}}$.

## Problem 12
*Tags: right-triangle, pythagorean-theorem*

Point $B$ is due east of point $A$. Point $C$ is due north of point $B$. The distance between points $A$ and $C$ is $10\sqrt 2$, and $\angle BAC = 45^\circ$. Point $D$ is $20$ meters due north of point $C$. The distance $AD$ is between which two integers?

$\textbf{(A)}\ 30\ \text{and}\ 31 \qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 31\ \text{and}\ 32 \qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 32\ \text{and}\ 33 \qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 33\ \text{and}\ 34 \qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 34\ \text{and}\ 35$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Find $AB$ and $BC$.** Since $B$ is east of $A$ and $C$ is north of $B$, $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$. With $\angle BAC = 45^\circ$, $\triangle ABC$ is $45$-$45$-$90$: $AB = BC = \frac{AC}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{10\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = 10$.

**Find $AD$.** $D$ is on the line $BC$ extended north, $20$ above $C$. So $BD = BC + CD = 10 + 20 = 30$.
$\triangle ABD$ is right-angled at $B$: $AD = \sqrt{AB^2 + BD^2} = \sqrt{100 + 900} = \sqrt{1000}$.

**Between which integers.** $31^2 = 961, 32^2 = 1024$. $\sqrt{1000}$ is between $31$ and $32$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(B) } 31 \text{ and } 32}$.

</details>

## Problem 13
*Tags: rate-work, escalator*

It takes Clea 60 seconds to walk down an escalator when it is not operating, and only 24 seconds to walk down the escalator when it is operating. How many seconds does it take Clea to ride down the operating escalator when she just stands on it?

$ \textbf{(A)}\ 36\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 40\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 42\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 48\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 52 $

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Setup.** Let escalator length $= 1$ unit. Clea's walking speed: $\tfrac{1}{60}$. Combined (walk + escalator): $\tfrac{1}{24}$. Escalator alone: $\tfrac{1}{24} - \tfrac{1}{60}$.

**Compute.** Common denominator $120$: $\tfrac{5}{120} - \tfrac{2}{120} = \tfrac{3}{120} = \tfrac{1}{40}$.

**Time standing.** $\tfrac{1}{1/40} = 40$ seconds.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(B) } 40}$.

</details>

## Problem 14
*Tags: equilateral-triangle-intersection, rhombus-area*

Two equilateral triangles are contained in square whose side length is $2\sqrt 3$. The bases of these triangles are the opposite side of the square, and their intersection is a rhombus. What is the area of the rhombus?

$
\text{(A) } \frac{3}{2}
\qquad
\text{(B) } \sqrt 3
\qquad
\text{(C) } 2\sqrt 2 -   1
\qquad
\text{(D) } 8\sqrt 3 - 12
\qquad
\text{(E)}  \frac{4\sqrt 3}{3}
$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Setup.** Square has side $s = 2\sqrt{3}$. Equilateral triangle with side $s$ has height $\tfrac{s\sqrt{3}}{2} = \tfrac{2\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}}{2} = 3$.

**Two triangles from opposite sides.** One has its base on the bottom, apex upward at height $3$; the other has its base on the top, apex downward at height $2\sqrt{3} - 3$.

Since the square's height is $2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.46$, the two triangles overlap in a rhombus that spans heights $2\sqrt{3} - 3$ to $3$.

**Rhombus diagonals.** The vertical diagonal (connecting the two apexes... wait, these are the apexes of the triangles, which are pointing toward each other): vertical length = $3 - (2\sqrt{3} - 3) = 6 - 2\sqrt{3}$.

**Horizontal diagonal.** The horizontal diagonal lies at the midline between the two apex heights, where both triangles have the same width. By the geometry of $60$-$30$-$90$ triangles: the top triangle narrows linearly from $2\sqrt{3}$ at the bottom to $0$ at height $3$; at height $y$ from the bottom, its width is $2\sqrt{3} \cdot (1 - y/3) \cdot 2/\sqrt{3}$... let me just use a cleaner approach.

By symmetry, the rhombus is formed by $4$ congruent $30$-$60$-$90$ triangles. Each has short leg = the horizontal half-diagonal, long leg = half the vertical diagonal.

The vertical half-diagonal: $\tfrac{6 - 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 3 - \sqrt{3}$.
In the $30$-$60$-$90$ configuration (where the long leg is opposite the $60°$ angle, which is the angle of the equilateral triangle's side from vertical), the horizontal half-diagonal = vertical-half-diagonal $/ \sqrt{3}$ = $(3 - \sqrt{3})/\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{3} - 1$.

**Rhombus area.** $\tfrac{1}{2} d_1 d_2 = \tfrac{1}{2} (6 - 2\sqrt{3})(2(\sqrt{3} - 1)) = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2(3 - \sqrt{3}) \cdot 2(\sqrt{3} - 1)$
$= 2 (3 - \sqrt{3})(\sqrt{3} - 1) = 2(3\sqrt{3} - 3 - 3 + \sqrt{3}) = 2(4\sqrt{3} - 6) = 8\sqrt{3} - 12$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(D) } 8\sqrt{3} - 12}$.

</details>

## Problem 15
*Tags: round-robin, tie-counting*

In a round-robin tournament with 6 teams, each team plays one game against each other team, and each game results in one team winning and one team losing. At the end of the tournament, the teams are ranked by the number of games won. What is the maximum number of teams that could be tied for the most wins at the end of the tournament?

$ \textbf{(A)}\ 2\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 3\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 4\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 5\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 6 $

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Total games.** $\binom{6}{2} = 15$, so $15$ total wins distributed.

**Can $6$ tie?** All six with equal wins requires $15/6 = 2.5$ — not an integer. ✗

**Can $5$ tie at the top?** Need $5$ teams each with $w$ wins (max), $1$ team with $< w$ wins. Total: $5w + (\text{one team's wins}) = 15$. Try $w = 3$: $5 \cdot 3 + 0 = 15$ — the sixth team has $0$ wins. Possible configuration: the sixth team loses to all others; the other $5$ teams play a round-robin among themselves (giving $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ games), and we need each to have $2$ wins among these. Each team among the $5$ plays $4$ intra-games; with a cyclic schedule each wins $2$ and loses $2$. ✓

So $5$ teams can tie.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(D) } 5}$.

</details>

## Problem 16
*Tags: tangent-circles, sectors*

Three circles with radius 2 are mutually tangent. What is the total area of the circles and the region bounded by them, as shown in the figure?

_(Asymptote diagram omitted — see AoPS for image.)_

$\textbf{(A)}\ 10\pi+4\sqrt{3}\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 13\pi-\sqrt{3}\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 12\pi+\sqrt{3}\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 10\pi+9\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 13\pi$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Setup.** Three unit-radius-$2$ circles mutually tangent. Centers form an equilateral triangle of side $4$ (distance between centers of externally tangent circles $= $ sum of radii $= 4$).

**Area of the triangle connecting centers.** $\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(4)^2 = 4\sqrt{3}$.

**Sectors of the circles inside the triangle.** At each vertex of the triangle, the angle is $60°$, and the sector inside is $\tfrac{60}{360} = \tfrac{1}{6}$ of that circle. Total: $3 \cdot \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ circle $= \tfrac{1}{2} \pi (2)^2 = 2\pi$.

**Area of curved triangle (the gap inside the three circles).** Triangle minus the three sectors: $4\sqrt{3} - 2\pi$.

**Total area (three circles + curved triangle).** $3 \cdot \pi(2)^2 + (4\sqrt{3} - 2\pi) = 12\pi + 4\sqrt{3} - 2\pi = 10\pi + 4\sqrt{3}$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(A) } 10\pi + 4\sqrt{3}}$.

</details>

## Problem 17
*Tags: cone, sector, volume*

Jesse cuts a circular paper disk of radius 12 along two radii to form two sectors, the smaller having a central angle of 120 degrees. He makes two circular cones, using each sector to form the lateral surface of a cone. What is the ratio of the volume of the smaller cone to that of the larger?

$\text{(A)} \frac{1}{8} \qquad \text{(B)} \frac{1}{4} \qquad \text{(C)} \frac{\sqrt{10}}{10} \qquad \text{(D)} \frac{\sqrt{5}}{6} \qquad \text{(E)} \frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Setup.** Each sector becomes a cone whose slant height is the disk's radius $= 12$, and whose base circumference equals the sector's arc length.

**Smaller cone ($120°$ sector).** Arc length $= \tfrac{120}{360} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 12 = 8\pi$, so base radius $r_1 = 4$. Slant $= 12$. Height $h_1 = \sqrt{12^2 - 4^2} = \sqrt{128} = 8\sqrt{2}$. Volume $V_1 = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r_1^2 h_1 = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi (16)(8\sqrt{2}) = \tfrac{128\sqrt{2}\pi}{3}$.

**Larger cone ($240°$ sector).** Arc length $= \tfrac{240}{360} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 12 = 16\pi$, so $r_2 = 8$. $h_2 = \sqrt{144 - 64} = \sqrt{80} = 4\sqrt{5}$. Volume $V_2 = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi (64)(4\sqrt{5}) = \tfrac{256\sqrt{5}\pi}{3}$.

**Ratio.** $\tfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \tfrac{128\sqrt{2}}{256\sqrt{5}} = \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2\sqrt{5}} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}} = \tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{10}$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(C) } \tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{10}}$.

</details>

## Problem 18
*Tags: conditional-probability, bayes*

Suppose that one of every 500 people in a certain population has a particular disease, which displays no symptoms. A blood test is available for screening for this disease. For a person who has this disease, the test always turns out positive. For a person who does not have the disease, however, there is a $2\%$ false positive rate--in other words, for such people, $98\%$ of the time the test will turn out negative, but $2\%$ of the time the test will turn out positive and will incorrectly indicate that the person has the disease. Let $p$ be the probability that a person who is chosen at random from this population and gets a positive test result actually has the disease. Which of the following is closest to $p$?

$\textbf{(A)}\ \frac{1}{98}\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ \frac{1}{9}\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ \frac{1}{11}\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ \frac{49}{99}\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ \frac{98}{99}$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Use concrete numbers.** Consider $500$ people. $1$ has the disease and tests positive. Of the other $499$ people, $2\%$ test positive: $\approx 10$ false positives.

**Bayes.** Among positive tests ($\approx 11$), the fraction with the disease is $\tfrac{1}{1 + 10} = \tfrac{1}{11}$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(C) } \tfrac{1}{11}}$.

</details>

## Problem 19
*Tags: similar-triangles, rectangle*

In rectangle $ABCD$, $AB=6$, $AD=30$, and $G$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AD}$. Segment $AB$ is extended 2 units beyond $B$ to point $E$, and $F$ is the intersection of $\overline{ED}$ and $\overline{BC}$. What is the area of quadrilateral $BFDG$?

$\textbf{(A)}\ \frac{133}{2}\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 67\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ \frac{135}{2}\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 68\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ \frac{137}{2}$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Coordinates.** $A = (0, 30), B = (6, 30), C = (6, 0), D = (0, 0)$. $G = $ midpoint of $AD$ $= (0, 15)$. $E = B + (2, 0) = (8, 30)$.

**Find $F$ on line $ED$ and $BC$.** Line $ED$ from $(8, 30)$ to $(0, 0)$: slope $30/8 = \tfrac{15}{4}$, equation $y = \tfrac{15}{4} x$. Line $BC$: $x = 6$. Intersection: $y = \tfrac{15}{4} \cdot 6 = \tfrac{45}{2}$. So $F = (6, \tfrac{45}{2})$.

**$BFDG$ is a trapezoid** with parallel sides $BG$ (vertical along $x = 0$ and $x = 6$... wait, let me check):
- $B = (6, 30), F = (6, \tfrac{45}{2}), D = (0, 0), G = (0, 15)$.

$BF$ is vertical at $x = 6$; $DG$ is vertical at $x = 0$. So $BF \parallel DG$, and the trapezoid has parallel vertical sides of lengths $BF = 30 - \tfrac{45}{2} = \tfrac{15}{2}$ and $DG = 15 - 0 = 15$. Distance between: $6$.

**Area.** $\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{15}{2} + 15) \cdot 6 = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \tfrac{45}{2} \cdot 6 = \tfrac{135}{2}$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(C) } \tfrac{135}{2}}$.

## Problem 20
*Tags: iterated-game, work-backwards*

Bernardo and Silvia play the following game. An integer between $0$ and $999$ inclusive is selected and given to Bernardo. Whenever Bernardo receives a number, he doubles it and passes the result to Silvia. Whenever Silvia receives a number, she adds $50$ to it and passes the result to Bernardo. The winner is the last person who produces a number less than $1000$. Let $N$ be the smallest initial number that results in a win for Bernardo. What is the sum of the digits of $N$?

$ \textbf{(A)}\ 7\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 8\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 9\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 10\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 11 $

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Track Bernardo's outputs starting from $N$.**
- Start: $N$ (Bernardo receives).
- After Bernardo doubles: $2N$ (Silvia receives).
- Silvia adds $50$: $2N + 50$ (Bernardo receives).
- Bernardo: $4N + 100$.
- Silvia: $4N + 150$.
- Bernardo: $8N + 300$.
- Silvia: $8N + 350$.
- Bernardo: $16N + 700$.
- Silvia: $16N + 750$.
- Bernardo: $32N + 1500$ — now $\ge 1000$, so Bernardo's output is too big.

**Bernardo wins if his last in-range output was $16N + 700$ (between $0$ and $999$), then Silvia's $16N + 750$ is also $\le 999$ (she continues), and then Bernardo's next ($32 N + 1500$) is $\ge 1000$.**

So we need $16N + 700 \le 999$ (Bernardo succeeds), $16N + 750 \le 999$ (Silvia succeeds), and $32N + 1500 \ge 1000$ — always true for $N \ge 1$.

Wait we want Bernardo to be the LAST producer $<$ $1000$. Let me recheck: the sequence of outputs alternates B, S, B, S, .... For Bernardo to win, his output is the last one under $1000$, meaning Silvia's next output exceeds $999$.

If Bernardo outputs $16N + 700 \le 999$, then Silvia outputs $16 N + 750$. For Bernardo to win here, Silvia's output should be $\ge 1000$: $16N + 750 \ge 1000 \iff 16N \ge 250 \iff N \ge 16$.

Also need $16N + 700 \le 999 \iff N \le \tfrac{299}{16} \approx 18.68$, so $N \le 18$.

**Minimum $N = 16$.** Digit sum: $1 + 6 = 7$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(A) } 7}$.

</details>

## Problem 21
*Tags: triangle-inequality, 30-60-90*

Four distinct points are arranged on a plane so that the segments connecting them have lengths $a$, $a$, $a$, $a$, $2a$, and $b$. What is the ratio of $b$ to $a$?

$\textbf{(A)}\ \sqrt{3}\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 2\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ \sqrt{5}\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 3\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ \pi$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Key observation.** Among the $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ distances between $4$ points, one is $2a$ and four are $a$. If any three points (triangle) had sides $(a, a, 2a)$, that violates triangle inequality ($a + a = 2a$, degenerate), so such a triangle would have to be colinear.

**Structural constraint.** The $2a$-length segment: its two endpoints must be colinear with a third point such that the two other distances are $a, a$ (the midpoint configuration). So three collinear points exist with pairwise distances $a, a, 2a$.

**Fourth point.** The fourth point forms distances with the three colinear points. Remaining edges: $a, a, b$. Two of the fourth point's distances are $a$, and the third is $b$.

**Geometry.** Place the three colinear points at $(-a, 0), (0, 0), (a, 0)$. The fourth point $P$ has distance $a$ to two of these. Possibilities:
- $P$ at distance $a$ from $(-a, 0)$ and $(0, 0)$: $P$ is at intersection of two circles of radius $a$ — at $(-a/2, \pm a\sqrt{3}/2)$. Distance to $(a, 0)$: $\sqrt{(3a/2)^2 + (a\sqrt{3}/2)^2} = \sqrt{9a^2/4 + 3a^2/4} = \sqrt{3 a^2} = a\sqrt{3}$. So $b = a\sqrt{3}$.
- Other pairings give the same result by symmetry, or give invalid configurations.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(A) } \sqrt{3}}$.

</details>

## Problem 22
*Tags: binomial-coefficients, ordering-sequences*

Let ($a_1$, $a_2$, ... $a_{10}$) be a list of the first 10 positive integers such that for each $2\le$ $i$ $\le10$ either $a_i + 1$ or $a_i-1$ or both appear somewhere before $a_i$ in the list. How many such lists are there?

$\textbf{(A)}\ \ 120\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 512\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ \ 1024\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 181,440\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ \ 362,880$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Condition.** Each number $a_i$ (for $i \ge 2$) must have a neighbor (in value) already placed before it.

**Reformulate as a connected-prefix property.** At each step, the numbers placed so far form a contiguous interval (a connected subset of $\{1, 2, \ldots, 10\}$). Once we've placed a contiguous interval, the next number must extend it on one end.

**Counting.** After the first number is placed, at each step we have $2$ choices (extend left or extend right), except when we've reached an end of $\{1, \ldots, 10\}$. But actually, we have exactly $9$ subsequent placements and at each we choose "left" or "right", but once one end is "pinned" (we reach $1$ or $10$), we're forced.

Alternative count: the number of such sequences equals the number of ways to choose, at each of the $9$ additions, whether to add the smallest-yet-unplaced-above or the largest-yet-unplaced-below... it's equivalent to binary choices at each step.

**Cleaner model.** The first number $k \in \{1, \ldots, 10\}$ can be any value. For a fixed first number $k$, after placement, the remaining $9$ numbers must be placed in order such that each extends the contiguous interval. At each of the $9$ steps, there are at most $2$ choices (left or right), but choices are forced when one side is exhausted.

By a clean bijection: each valid sequence corresponds to a binary string of length $9$ (at each step: $0$ = extend left, $1$ = extend right, with forced choices when appropriate). Total sequences: $2^9 = 512$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(B) } 512}$.

</details>

## Problem 23
*Tags: 3d-geometry, tetrahedron-cut*

A solid tetrahedron is sliced off a solid wooden unit cube by a plane passing through two nonadjacent vertices on one face and one vertex on the opposite face not adjacent to either of the first two vertices. The tetrahedron is discarded and the remaining portion of the cube is placed on a table with the cut surface face down. What is the height of this object?

$ \textbf{(A)}\ \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \qquad\textbf{(B)}\ \frac{2 \sqrt{2}}{3}\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 1\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ \frac{2 \sqrt{3}}{3}\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ \sqrt{2} $

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Setup.** Place the unit cube with vertices at $(0,0,0)$ through $(1,1,1)$. The cut plane passes through $3$ vertices with the given nonadjacency properties — e.g., $(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1)$ (two vertices diagonally opposite on the bottom face and one on the top face).

These three vertices form an equilateral triangle with side $\sqrt{2}$ (pairwise distances all equal).

**The remaining solid** is the cube minus a corner tetrahedron (with the corner $(0, 0, 0)$ and the three vertices listed).

**Place the cut face on the table.** The cut face (equilateral triangle with side $\sqrt{2}$) is now horizontal. We need the height from this plane to the farthest point of the remaining solid.

**Farthest point.** The vertex $(1, 1, 1)$ is diagonally opposite $(0, 0, 0)$ (the corner removed). The cut plane passes through the three neighbors of $(0, 0, 0)$. The body diagonal from $(0, 0, 0)$ to $(1, 1, 1)$ has length $\sqrt{3}$, and the cut plane intersects it at its centroid point, which is $1/3$ of the way from $(0, 0, 0)$ to $(1, 1, 1)$.

So distance from cut plane to $(1, 1, 1)$ is $\tfrac{2}{3} \sqrt{3} = \tfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(D) } \tfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}}$.

</details>

## Problem 24
*Tags: casework, venn-diagram*

Amy, Beth, and Jo listen to four different songs and discuss which ones they like. No song is liked by all three. Furthermore, for each of the three pairs of the girls, there is at least one song liked by those two girls but disliked by the third. In how many different ways is this possible?

$ \textbf{(A)}\ 108\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 132\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 671\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 846\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 1105 $

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Categorize each song.** Each of the $4$ songs falls into one of these categories, based on which subset of $\{A, B, J\}$ likes it (excluding $\{A, B, J\}$ itself since no song is liked by all three):
- $\emptyset$: disliked by all.
- $\{A\}, \{B\}, \{J\}$: liked by exactly one.
- $\{A, B\}, \{B, J\}, \{A, J\}$: liked by exactly two.

That's $7$ categories per song.

**Constraint.** For each of the three pairs, at least one song is in that pair's category.

**Inclusion-exclusion.** Let $X = \{A, B\}, Y = \{B, J\}, Z = \{A, J\}$ be the three "pair" categories. We need each of $X, Y, Z$ to contain at least one song.

Total labelings (ignoring constraint): $7^4$. Subtract those missing $X$: $6^4$. Similarly for $Y, Z$. Add back those missing two pairs, etc.:
$$N = 7^4 - 3 \cdot 6^4 + 3 \cdot 5^4 - 4^4 = 2401 - 3888 + 1875 - 256 = 132.$$

The answer is $\boxed{\textbf{(B) } 132}$.

</details>
